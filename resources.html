Readiness

§ 1746.5 Checklist

All of these must be documented and available for Board inspection before you furnish a single travel med.

  • Immunization training program — § 4052.8(b)(1) You have this. Keep the certificate on file.
  • 10-hour travel-medicine training (ISTM Body of Knowledge) You have this. Keep the certificate on file.
  • CDC Yellow Fever Vaccine Course Explicitly required by § 1746.5(c)(3) even though you refer YF out. Confirm completion or take it before launch.
  • Current Basic Life Support (BLS) certification Required by § 1746.5(c)(4). Confirm it's active.
  • 2 hours travel-medicine CE every 2 years Required by § 1746.5(d), separate from immunization CE. This is the one you flagged — complete before furnishing.
  • Individual Type 1 NPI Free at nppes.cms.hhs.gov. Required to be identified as the furnisher on any transmitted order.
  • 1-hour opioid-antagonist (naloxone) CE — § 1746.3 Required before furnishing naloxone under the statewide protocol (BPC § 4052.01). Take the 1-hour furnishing course, not the broad opioid-safety course. Keep the certificate on file.
The Core Question

Furnishing From Your Laptop to CVS

You are not dispensing — you have no pharmacy permit or inventory. You furnish (clinically authorize) and transmit the order to the patient's chosen retail pharmacy, which dispenses. Under BPC § 4024, "dispense" includes furnishing upon an order from a pharmacist acting within scope, so a retail pharmacy is legally permitted to fill your furnishing order.

1. Get credentialed

Individual Type 1 NPI (free). None of the standard travel meds are controlled substances, so you do not need a DEA number or EPCS identity-proofing — this keeps setup simple.

2. Get an e-Rx tool

A Surescripts-connected e-prescribing tool where you enroll as the prescriber/furnisher: standalone (DoseSpot, ScriptSure, iPrescribe) or inside an EHR (Practice Fusion ~$199/mo, DrChrono). This is the laptop-to-pharmacy pipe.

3. Transmit the order

In the tool, pick the patient's pharmacy by name/address, select the drug + sig + qty, and send. Your name, California license number, and NPI ride on the order. CVS receives it like any prescriber order.

Backup path: If e-Rx isn't set up yet, CA also allows a written or faxed furnishing order (16 CCR § 1717, BPC § 4040/§ 4070) containing all standard Rx elements. Note CA's e-prescribing mandate (AB 2789) makes electronic the default, with documented exceptions — so stand up the e-Rx tool as the primary method. Practical friction: some retail techs haven't seen a pharmacist-furnished travel order. A one-line note citing "Furnished per BPC § 4052(a)(10)(A)(3)" and a quick call to the pharmacy clears it up fast.

Per-Patient Workflow

Every Furnish, Same Steps

1

Good-faith eval

Travel history + destination-specific risk assessment per CDC Yellow Book — § 1746.5(e).

2

Furnish + transmit

Authorize eligible meds and e-send to the patient's pharmacy under your NPI.

3

Notify PCP (14 days)

Notify the PCP within 14 days, or give the patient a written record if none — § 1746.5(f).

4

Document

Keep a patient medication record + give the patient a written clinical assessment and travel plan — § 1746.5(g).

Software Stack

Recommended Setup

Square Appointments — Booking + Prepay

All consults booked here. Free for solo. Collects prepayment by card at booking (same Square ecosystem as your payments). No PHI at the booking step — that goes to Charm — so no BAA needed on the scheduler.

Charm EHR — Records + Video

HIPAA-secure intake, the travel history, the visit (built-in video), and the patient medication record. Do not use Google Forms for any health info.

e-Rx — Transmission

DoseSpot/ScriptSure standalone, or e-Rx inside Practice Fusion/DrChrono. This is the only piece the old supplement/coaching plan didn't need — it's what makes furnishing real.

Compliance guardrails: (1) CA patients only — confirm the patient is physically in California at the time of service. (2) Furnish only CDC-recommended travel meds that don't require a diagnosis; anything diagnostic is out of scope without a CPA. (3) Keep the EliteMetabolix coaching brand on a separate domain with its own non-clinical disclaimers — never cross-link it from the licensed travel-health pages, which is exactly the consumer-confusion concern the Board hinted at. (4) Consider a healthcare attorney review of the furnishing protocol + intake language before the first paid furnish.
